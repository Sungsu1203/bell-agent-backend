### Findings

1. **Global Economic Growth Trends:**
   - Global growth is projected to remain stable at 3.3% for 2025 and 2026, with slight acceleration in advanced economies [IMF].
   - However, some forecasts indicate a slowdown to 2.9% in 2025 and 2026 due to trade policy uncertainties [OECD].

2. **Impact of Climate Change:**
   - Long-term economic projections highlight significant effects of climate change on GDP, with potential negative impacts on output [CBO].
   - Gender inequality and climate change are interconnected, affecting economic and social structures [UN Women].

3. **Trade and Geopolitical Risks:**
   - The 2025 trade war is expected to have dynamic impacts across U.S. states and the global economy, contributing to financial market volatility [FRBSF, McKinsey].
   - Geopolitical risks are influencing asset prices and could weigh on economic growth [IMF].

4. **Inflation and Consumer Prices:**
   - Inflation rates vary significantly across regions, with some areas experiencing high consumer price changes [IMF Data Mapper].

### Evidence Summary

- The IMF projects stable global growth rates, but the OECD anticipates a slowdown due to trade uncertainties. The CBO's working paper discusses the long-term economic impacts of climate change, which could affect GDP. The UN Women article highlights the link between gender inequality and climate change. The FRBSF and McKinsey reports discuss the economic consequences of the 2025 trade war and its impact on financial markets. The IMF blog and data mapper provide insights into geopolitical risks and inflation trends.

### Implications/Additional Research Points

   - Further analysis is needed to understand the differing growth projections between the IMF and OECD, particularly in the context of trade policy impacts.

2. **Explore Mitigation Strategies for Climate Change:**
   - Research effective economic policies and strategies to mitigate the negative impacts of climate change on GDP.

3. **Assess the Long-term Effects of Trade Wars:**
   - Examine the long-term economic consequences of trade wars on both domestic and global scales, focusing on potential policy responses.
