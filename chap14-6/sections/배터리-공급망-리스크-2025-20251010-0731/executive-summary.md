## 1. Executive Summary

The global battery supply chain is facing significant challenges as we approach 2025, driven by geopolitical tensions, resource scarcity, and increasing demand for electric vehicles and renewable energy storage solutions. This executive summary provides a concise overview of the key risks and strategic considerations for stakeholders involved in the battery supply chain, highlighting the need for proactive risk management and strategic planning.

### Background

The battery industry is critical to the global transition towards sustainable energy. However, the supply chain is vulnerable to various risks, including geopolitical conflicts, resource dependency, and market volatility. Countries like South Korea, which hold a significant share in the global battery market, are particularly exposed to these risks due to their reliance on imported raw materials such as lithium, cobalt, and nickel [KDI].[^1]

### Key Risks

1. **Resource Dependency**: The supply of essential raw materials is concentrated in a few countries, leading to potential supply disruptions. For instance, over 90% of certain materials like natural graphite and nickel ores are imported from specific countries, posing a significant risk to supply chain stability [KIET].[^2]

2. **Geopolitical Tensions**: The ongoing technological competition between major economies, such as the US and China, and regional conflicts can disrupt supply chains. The COVID-19 pandemic has further highlighted vulnerabilities in global supply networks [KDI].[^1]

3. **Market Volatility**: Fluctuations in raw material prices can significantly impact production costs and profitability. The volatile nature of commodity markets requires robust financial strategies to mitigate risks [MOEF].

### Strategic Insights

- **Diversification**: Diversifying the sources of raw materials and investing in alternative materials can reduce dependency on specific countries. This strategy also involves exploring recycling and circular economy models to reclaim materials from used batteries.

- **Technological Innovation**: Advancements in battery technology, such as solid-state batteries, can reduce reliance on scarce materials and improve supply chain resilience. Continued investment in research and development is crucial for maintaining competitive advantage.

- **Policy and Collaboration**: Governments and industry stakeholders must collaborate to develop policies that support supply chain resilience. This includes establishing strategic reserves, fostering international partnerships, and investing in domestic production capabilities [KDI].[^1]

### Actionable Recommendations

1. **Develop Strategic Partnerships**: Establish alliances with countries and companies to secure a stable supply of critical raw materials. This includes joint ventures and long-term contracts with suppliers.

2. **Invest in Recycling Technologies**: Promote and invest in battery recycling technologies to recover valuable materials and reduce dependency on virgin resources.

3. **Enhance Supply Chain Transparency**: Implement advanced tracking and monitoring systems to improve visibility across the supply chain, enabling quicker responses to disruptions.

4. **Advocate for Supportive Policies**: Engage with policymakers to advocate for regulations and incentives that support supply chain diversification and innovation.

5. **Focus on R&D**: Allocate resources to research and development of alternative battery technologies that reduce reliance on high-risk materials.

By addressing these key risks and implementing strategic recommendations, stakeholders can enhance the resilience of the battery supply chain, ensuring sustainable growth and stability in the face of future challenges.
